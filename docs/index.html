Topic 1 · Forecast & Replenishment Generation

How does CRS generate forecasts and replenishment recommendations at the distributor-SKU level?

Step 1 — Demand Classification (ADI/CV² Matrix):

  • ADI (Average Inter-Demand Interval) & CV² (Squared CoV) computed per SKU
  • ADI < 1.32 + CV² < 0.49 → ETS (smooth, fast-moving)
  • ADI ≥ 1.32 + CV² < 0.49 → SBA (intermittent)
  • ADI < 1.32 + CV² ≥ 0.49 → ARIMA (erratic)
  • ADI ≥ 1.32 + CV² ≥ 0.49 → SBA (lumpy)
  • Revenue-A or high covariate R² → TFT (neural, multi-horizon)

Step 2 — Syntetos-Boylan Approximation (SBA) for intermittent/lumpy:

f_SBA = (1 − α/2) × z̄ₜ / p̄ₜ

Debiases Croston's method by the factor (1−α/2) where α is the smoothing constant, z̄ₜ is the demand-size estimate, and p̄ₜ is the inter-demand interval estimate.


Step 3 — Target Stock Level (TSL):

TSL = D̄(T+L) + Z·√(L·σD² + D̄²·σL²)

T=review period, L=lead time, σD=demand std dev, σL=lead-time std dev, Z=service-level z-score. Includes lead-time variance decomposition.


Step 4 — ROQ with logistics constraints:

  • ROQ_raw = max(0, TSL − Net Inventory Position)
  • Apply MOQ floor → round up to case pack → pallet heuristic (round up if remainder ≥ 40% of pallet)
  • DOS ceiling cap: if projected DOS > 90 days → hard cut to prevent hoarding
Topic 2 · Override & Modification Workflow

At what stages can Sales, planners, or distributors override CRS-generated recommendations?

4 Override Entry Points:

  • Demand Planner: Adjusts forecast quantity in Exception Triage panel (P90 breach, negative FVA, model reclassification). Override range gated to ±15% of P50 to prevent arbitrary changes.
  • Sales / Executive: Revenue-target simulator adjusts network-level uplift applied proportionally across distributors using revenue tier weights.
  • Distributor: Requests quantity adjustments via portal (e.g., pre-promotional build-up, warehouse capacity constraints). Triggers re-approval workflow.
  • Credit Admin: Manual credit-hold release unblocks distributors from CREDIT_HOLD status, allowing solver to process their allocations.

Override Accountability (Forecast Value Added — FVA):

FVA = MAPE_naive − MAPE_adjusted

Every manual override is logged with original_qty, overridden_qty, pct_change, reason_code, user_id, and FVA scores in audit_override_log. Negative FVA (override made forecast worse than the naive baseline) triggers an exception flag in the next cycle, visible to the Demand Planner.


Reason Codes tracked:

  • PROMOTIONAL_UPLIFT · COMPETITOR_DISRUPTION
  • SEASONAL_PRE_BUILD · ERRONEOUS_FORECAST
  • WAREHOUSE_CAPACITY · CREDIT_CONSTRAINT

Temporal.io Human-in-the-Loop Gate: After ROQ calculation and before ERP release, the saga pauses at APPROVAL_WAIT. A senior planner can approve or reject via API signal. Rejection triggers compensating transactions to reverse any ERP credit holds already placed.

Topic 3 · Fair-Share Credit Allocation

When a distributor's credit is insufficient, how does CRS allocate the available amount across SKUs?

OR-Tools MILP Formulation (SCIP backend, <2ms solve time):

Maximise: z_min (minimum DOS across all eligible SKUs)
Subject to: Σ (x_i × unit_cost_i) ≤ credit_available
∀i: x_i × demand_i − z_min × demand_i ≥ −NIP_i (DOS ≥ z_min)
∀i Tier-1: x_i ≥ 0.90 × ROQ_i (90% floor reservation)

Where x_i = allocated order quantity, z_min = auxiliary continuous variable = minimum DOS any SKU achieves.


Tier Prioritisation:

  • Tier 1 (A-class revenue): 90% floor reservation — never cut below 90% of their ROQ regardless of credit pressure
  • Tier 2 (B-class): Participate in fair-share allocation pool
  • Tier 3 (C-class): Excluded from allocation when credit < 60% of total ROQ value

What "fair share" means: Rather than first-come-first-served or proportional-cut, the MILP finds the allocation that maximises the worst-off distributor's DOS. If two distributors have different demand rates, they receive different quantities but identical Days-of-Stock coverage — genuinely equitable, not just proportional.


Output logged to: credit_allocation_ledger with solver_status (WITHIN_LIMIT / CREDIT_HOLD / FAIR_SHARE_APPLIED), solver_run_ms, and allocated_order_val per distributor-SKU batch.

OR-Tools MILP — How the Solver Works

# Google OR-Tools SCIP backend — Fair-Share Allocation
solver = cp_model.CpModel() # MILP, not CP — uses SCIP

# Objective: maximise the minimum DOS any SKU achieves
z_min = solver.NewIntVar(0, 90, 'z_min') # DOS ceiling = 90 days

for sku in tier_1_skus:
  # Tier-1 floor: allocate ≥ 90% of recommended ROQ
  solver.Add(x[sku] >= 0.90 * sku.roq)

for sku in all_eligible_skus:
  # DOS constraint: allocation + NIP must achieve at least z_min DOS
  solver.Add(x[sku] * sku.demand - z_min * sku.demand >= -sku.nip)

# Budget constraint: total order value ≤ credit available
solver.Add(sum(x[s] * s.unit_cost for s in skus) <= credit_available)

solver.Maximize(z_min)

Key Talking Points for Each Client Question:

Q1 (Forecast generation): "The system classifies every SKU-distributor pair on the ADI/CV² matrix each week. High-value SKUs get the TFT model with probabilistic P10/P50/P90 bands. Intermittent-demand SKUs use SBA, which debiases the classic Croston method by a factor of (1−α/2). The Target Stock Level formula explicitly accounts for lead-time variance, not just demand variance."

Q2 (Overrides): "Any of the 4 personas can influence the replenishment — but every change is logged with the original quantity, the new quantity, the reason code, and the user ID. More importantly, the system calculates Forecast Value Added: if your override made the forecast worse than a naive baseline, you get flagged on the next cycle. This creates accountability without restricting human judgment."

Q3 (Fair-share credit): "We use a MILP solver from Google OR-Tools. The objective is to maximise the minimum Days-of-Stock any SKU achieves — not to cut proportionally. Tier-1 SKUs get a 90% floor, so strategic products are never starved. The solver runs in under 5 milliseconds for a 30-SKU batch, so it fits inside the replenishment cycle runtime."
